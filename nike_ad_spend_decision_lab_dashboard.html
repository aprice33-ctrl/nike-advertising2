Decision guidance for companies in the same sector

1. Spend more only with a clear return target

In footwear and apparel, brand investment matters, but extra spend should be linked to measurable demand: traffic, conversion, sell-through, and margin quality.

2. Protect margin while chasing growth

Advertising that requires heavy discounts can lift short-term sales but weaken profit. The best campaigns increase demand without training customers to wait for sales.

3. Tie marketing to shareholder outcomes

Leadership should report whether campaigns are improving revenue, profitability, and cash available for dividends or buybacks—not just impressions and brand awareness.

    Customer-facing conclusion

    The Nike case is useful because it is not a simple “more ads = more value” story. In FY2025, demand creation expense increased while sales and profit declined. For companies in the same sector, the right advice is selective investment: fund campaigns that protect brand strength, improve full-price demand, and produce cash—not campaigns that only buy short-term attention.
